Almost everyone experiences back pain at some point in their lives. Often, it's just a minor muscle tweak from lifting something heavy or sleeping in an awkward position. In these cases, a few days of rest and gentle movement might be all you need.

But how do you know when back pain is no longer a DIY fix? When is it time to pick up the phone and schedule an appointment with a physiotherapist?

## 1. The Pain Lasts Longer Than a Few Days
Acute back pain usually subsides within a few days to a week. If you've been resting, applying heat or ice, and the pain is still persistent or worsening after 3-5 days, it’s a clear sign that you should seek professional assessment.

## 2. The Pain Radiates Down Your Legs
If your back pain is accompanied by a shooting or burning pain that travels down your glutes, thighs, or calves, you might be dealing with nerve compression, commonly known as **Sciatica**. A physiotherapist can perform specific tests to determine the root cause of this radiating pain and provide targeted relief.

## 3. You Experience Numbness or Tingling
Numbness or a "pins and needles" sensation in your legs or feet is another strong indicator of nerve involvement. This should not be ignored. A thorough physiotherapy assessment will help decompress the affected nerve safely.

## 4. The Pain is Severely Limiting Your Mobility
If you cannot bend over to tie your shoes, struggle to stand up straight, or find it impossible to sit at your desk for more than 10 minutes, you need professional intervention. Physiotherapy focuses on restoring your function so you can get back to your daily activities.
