Modern work often means spending eight or more hours sitting in front of laptops, monitors, and smartphones. Over time, holding your head forward—a condition commonly known as **"Tech Neck"**—places immense mechanical strain on the cervical spine and surrounding muscles.

For every inch your head drifts forward past neutral alignment, the cervical spine experiences an extra 4.5 to 5 kg of weight. This leads to chronic upper back tightness, stiffness, and tension headaches.

Here are 5 simple, effective desk exercises recommended by Dr. Abhishek Kumar to restore cervical mobility and relieve tension throughout your workday.

---

## 1. Chin Tucks (Cervical Retraction)
Chin tucks are one of the most effective movements for strengthening deep neck flexors and reversing forward-head posture.

- **How to do it:** Sit upright with your back supported. Looking straight ahead, gently draw your chin straight back as if making a subtle "double chin".
- **Repetitions:** Hold for 3 to 5 seconds, release, and repeat 10 times.
- **Tip:** Do not tilt your head down; keep your eyes focused horizontally.

## 2. Shoulder Blade Squeezes (Scapular Retractions)
Long hours of typing cause the pectoral muscles to tighten while the upper back muscles become lengthened and weak.

- **How to do it:** Sit tall with arms relaxed at your sides. Pull both shoulder blades back and down toward each other, as if squeezing a pencil between your spine.
- **Repetitions:** Hold for 5 seconds and repeat 10 to 12 times.
- **Benefit:** Opens up the anterior chest wall and activates the mid-trapezius and rhomboids.

## 3. Upper Trapezius Stretch
The upper trapezius muscles carry a disproportionate amount of physical and mental stress throughout the workday.

- **How to do it:** Sit on your right hand to anchor your shoulder down. Gently bring your left ear toward your left shoulder until you feel a comfortable stretch along the right side of your neck.
- **Repetitions:** Hold for 20 to 30 seconds, breathe deeply, and switch sides.
- **Caution:** Never pull forcefully on your head; allow gentle guidance only.

## 4. Seated Thoracic Extension
Hunched sitting locks up the mid-back (thoracic spine), forcing the neck to compensate.

- **How to do it:** Place your hands behind your head with elbows pointing outward. Lean back over the top of your chair's backrest, looking slightly upward toward the ceiling.
- **Repetitions:** Hold for 3 to 5 seconds at the end-range and repeat 8 times.
- **Benefit:** Mobilizes the thoracic vertebrae and promotes upright lung expansion.

## 5. Levator Scapulae Stretch
The levator scapulae connects the cervical vertebrae to the shoulder blade and is often the primary culprit behind localized neck knots.

- **How to do it:** Turn your head 45 degrees to the left, and gently tuck your chin toward your left armpit. You will feel a targeted stretch down the opposite side of your neck.
- **Repetitions:** Hold for 20 to 30 seconds per side.

## Setting Up Your Workspace for Success
Exercise works best when paired with ergonomic adjustments:
1. **Screen Height:** The top third of your display should be at eye level.
2. **Elbow Angle:** Keep elbows bent at 90 degrees with forearms supported.
3. **The 30-Minute Rule:** Set a subtle reminder to stand up, stretch, or change positions every 30 to 45 minutes.

## When to Seek Professional Physiotherapy
If neck stiffness is accompanied by persistent headaches, radiating tingling into your shoulders or fingers, or doesn't improve with stretching, you should undergo a clinical assessment.
