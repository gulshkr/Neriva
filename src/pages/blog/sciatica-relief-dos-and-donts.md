Sciatica is not a disease in itself; it is a symptom of irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve—the longest and thickest nerve in the human body. 

Starting in your lower back and traveling deep through your buttock down into your foot, sciatic irritation can cause sharp, electric, burning pain, numbness, or tingling that makes even sitting down an ordeal.

When acute sciatica strikes, well-meaning advice from friends or the internet can sometimes make things significantly worse. Here are the crucial **Do's and Don'ts** guided by evidence-based orthopedic physiotherapy.

---

## The DON'TS (Common Mistakes to Avoid)

### 1. DON’T Stay in Bed All Day
Bed rest beyond 24 to 48 hours is one of the worst things you can do for sciatica. Prolonged lying down weakens stabilizing muscles, increases spinal stiffness, and impairs blood circulation to the inflamed nerve root.

### 2. DON’T Do Aggressive Hamstring Stretches
A very common instinct is to sit on the floor, reach for your toes, and stretch your tight leg. However, straight-leg forward bending places the sciatic nerve on extreme mechanical tension, severely irritating an already inflamed nerve.

### 3. DON’T Sit on Soft Couches or Deep Chairs
Sitting in low, plush sofas flexes the lumbar spine and pushes disc material backward toward the spinal nerve roots. Always choose a firm, upright chair with good lumbar support.

### 4. DON’T Ignore Warning Signs
If your sciatica is accompanied by sudden numbness in the groin area (saddle anesthesia) or loss of bowel/bladder control, this represents a medical emergency known as *Cauda Equina Syndrome* and requires immediate hospital attention.

---

## The DO'S (What Actually Helps)

### 1. DO Use Short, Frequent Walking Breaks
Gentle walking on flat surfaces promotes blood flow, releases natural endorphins, and encourages gentle spinal disc decompression. Aim for 10 to 15-minute walks multiple times a day rather than one long, exhausting walk.

### 2. DO Try the Prone Lying Position (McKenzie Extension)
Lying face-down on a firm mat for 3 to 5 minutes can help reduce posterior disc bulge pressure. If comfortable, you can gently prop up on your elbows (sphinx pose) to promote lumbar extension.

### 3. DO Alternate Ice and Heat Strategically
- **First 48-72 hours:** Apply an ice pack wrapped in a towel for 15-20 minutes to reduce local inflammatory swelling.
- **After 72 hours:** Use gentle moist heat to relax tense muscles in the gluteal and piriformis region.

### 4. DO Practice Nerve Flossing (Neural Glides)
Under physiotherapist guidance, gentle "nerve flossing" techniques help the sciatic nerve slide smoothly through its surrounding tissues rather than tugging on inflamed nerve roots.
